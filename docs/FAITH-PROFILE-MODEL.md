# Faith profile model

## Objective

Support meaningful compatibility for Muslim students and students from a Muslim background without ranking religious worth, inferring belief or forcing one definition of being Muslim.

## Design principles

- self-description only;
- descriptive categories, never a numeric religiosity score;
- background, practice and preference are separate dimensions;
- practical lifestyle tags remain optional;
- users control public visibility;
- private and prefer-not-to-say options remain available;
- no religion inference from name, image, origin, behavior or institution;
- no advertising use;
- no moral or sectarian ranking language.

## Prototype fields

### Faith background

- Muslim;
- Muslim background;
- convert;
- exploring/spiritual;
- prefer not to say.

### Daily practice

- actively practising;
- practising;
- moderately practising;
- culturally connected;
- prefer to keep private.

### Importance in a match

- essential;
- important;
- important but open;
- no strict preference.

### Optional lifestyle tags

- prayer matters;
- Ramadan matters;
- halal lifestyle;
- no alcohol;
- does not smoke;
- family-oriented;
- modesty matters;
- community-minded;
- marriage-minded.

## Public presentation

The discovery card should show at most a few faith/lifestyle indicators. It should not overwhelm the visual profile or turn compatibility into a checklist.

Public visibility rules:

- faith-practice visibility defaults off;
- the user deliberately enables it in the privacy step;
- private choices remain hidden;
- lifestyle tags are user-selected;
- detailed preference filters should not be visible to other users by default.

## Matching use

Faith data may support compatibility, but shall not be the only ranking signal.

Potential matching dimensions:

- relationship intention;
- importance of faith compatibility;
- practice openness;
- explicit lifestyle dealbreakers;
- city, age, education and shared interests.

The system shall avoid statements such as:

- “more religious match”;
- “better Muslim”;
- “low/high faith quality”;
- “religious compatibility score.”

## Production privacy requirements

Before real users:

- document the Article 9 processing condition;
- obtain explicit and separable choice where relied upon;
- explain purposes in Dutch and English;
- allow field-level withdrawal, editing and deletion;
- define retention and backup deletion;
- restrict staff access;
- audit access and exports;
- conduct a DPIA;
- prevent ad targeting, sale and unrelated analytics;
- test harassment, exclusion and coercion risks;
- ensure account deletion removes faith fields according to policy.

## Research protocol

Validate with a diverse sample including:

- actively practising students;
- moderately practising students;
- culturally connected students;
- converts;
- students who prefer not to display practice;
- MBO, HBO and WO students;
- multiple genders and cultural backgrounds.

Questions:

1. Are the labels understandable and respectful?
2. Do users feel pressured to present themselves as more religious?
3. Are background and daily practice sufficiently distinct?
4. Which fields materially improve compatibility?
5. Which fields feel invasive or unsafe?
6. Should any field become optional or move behind progressive disclosure?
7. Does private-by-default visibility create enough control?
8. Can dealbreakers be expressed without moral judgement?

## Stop criteria

Redesign or remove fields if research shows:

- users feel ranked or shamed;
- categories create material sectarian conflict;
- disclosure pressure outweighs matching value;
- sensitive data cannot be processed proportionately;
- the model encourages exclusion unrelated to user-stated compatibility;
- moderation cannot control targeted harassment.
