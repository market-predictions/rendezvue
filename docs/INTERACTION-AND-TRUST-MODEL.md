# Interaction, feedback and trust model

**Version:** 1.0  
**Updated:** 2026-07-29

## 1. Four separate systems

Rendezvue separates:

1. **Attraction signals** — pass, like and contextual like;
2. **Private experience feedback** — structured reflection after meaningful contact;
3. **Safety reports** — suspected rule or safety violations;
4. **Internal trust signals** — evidence-backed patterns used for guarded interventions.

Combining these into one rating would turn personal taste and rejection into an unfair reputation score.

## 2. Likes and passes

A like means “I may want to know this person.” It is not proof of reliability, morality or suitability for others. A pass is private preference and never lowers general visibility.

Supported actions:

- pass;
- direct like;
- contextual like attached to a prompt or profile component;
- reciprocal match.

Public like/pass counts, popularity scores and ranking by raw attraction volume are prohibited.

## 3. Contact opening

A reciprocal match creates contact potential, not necessarily an open conversation. One participant can use a contact entitlement to open it. Both participants can then reply without per-message charges.

The concept pilot simulates one contact right and shows indicative regular/student pricing. It processes no payment.

## 4. Private feedback

Feedback is requested only after meaningful interaction or explicit contact ending. It uses concrete tags rather than stars.

Positive examples:

- respectful;
- honest profile;
- serious intent;
- respected boundaries.

Concern examples:

- little response;
- pressure;
- quickly moving off-platform;
- money request;
- may not be single;
- offensive language;
- no-show.

“No chemistry, no problem” is neutral.

## 5. Influence on profile standing

### No effect

- pass rate;
- a single negative review;
- incompatibility or lack of attraction;
- popularity volume.

### First response to a reliable non-safety pattern

1. no silent penalty;
2. private profile or intent refresh prompt;
3. temporary contact-opening limit when proportionate;
4. explainable reduced distribution only after a robust pattern and review;
5. appeal route.

### Serious signals

Scam, threats, hidden relationship, suspected minor, stalking or sexual harassment create a moderation case. They are not handled as an ordinary ranking adjustment.

## 6. Public presentation

No stars, public thumbs, negative badges or numerical user scores. Only sufficiently supported positive badges may eventually be displayed, such as recent verification or sustained respectful contact.

## 7. Bias and retaliation controls

Feedback weight should consider interaction depth, reviewer reliability, timing after rejection, correlated accounts, mutual conflict and consistency with independent evidence. Signals decay over time. Fairness monitoring must test whether particular demographic, family or faith groups receive systematically different outcomes.

## 8. Pilot boundary

The current static pilot stores structured feedback locally for UX testing. It has no ranking, visibility or enforcement effect. This is deliberate until real data, moderation operations and fairness safeguards exist.
